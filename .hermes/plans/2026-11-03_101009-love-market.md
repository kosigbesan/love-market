# Love Market (Ọjà Ìfẹ́), Build Plan

**Goal:** Launch a Nigeria-native dating app that wins where Tinder and Bumble are structurally weak: trust, safety, payment friction, and cultural fit.

**One-line thesis:** Tinder sells access to strangers. Love Market sells *verified* strangers you can safely meet, and it takes naira, on any phone, over any network.

**Working name:** Ọjà Ìfẹ́ ("love market"). Market metaphor is load-bearing: a market is a place with traders, reputations, and witnesses. That is the whole product.

---

## 1. Why Tinder is beatable here (the actual gap)

Not the swipe. The swipe is fine. The gaps are:

| Gap | What actually happens in Nigeria | Love Market's answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust** | Catfishing, "runs girls", sugar-daddy scams, romance fraud. Reddit's own verdict on NG options: "both have been occupied by SW". | Phone-first identity + NIN/BVN + liveness selfie before you can appear in a deck. |
| **Safety** | Women carry kidnapping/robbery risk on a first meet. No app addresses it locally. | Women message first (optional), in-app voice/video (no premature WhatsApp handoff), date check-in with a trusted contact, live location share. |
| **Payment** | Cards are rare, fail on FX, and recurring auth charges are distrusted. | Naira pricing, bank transfer, USSD, and **airtime** via Paystack/Flutterwave. Wallet credits instead of silent card re-auths. |
| **Data** | ~40% mobile-internet penetration, expensive metered data, 2G/3G moments. | Low-data mode default, aggressive photo compression, voice notes as first-class (cheaper than scrolling photos). |
| **Culture** | Courtship is semi-public. "What will people say" is real. Hookup framing alienates a huge serious segment. | Intention tags up front ("Serious", "Friendship", "Still figuring it out"). Voice-note openers, not one-liners. |

**Market numbers that make this worth doing:** smartphone ownership rose 64% → 75% (2023 to 2025, KPMG/Orange), Android holds ~63% of that base, and mobile internet penetration is still only ~40%, the second half of the market is *arriving*, not saturated. Tinder is used largely by the Lagos/Abuja elite and via Passport mode. The mass market is unserved.

---

## 2. The five decisions that define the product

1. **Verification is the product, not a feature.** If verification is optional you have built Tinder with a green tick. Make it the gate: unverified profiles are shadow-ranked and cannot be swiped right.
2. **Safety is never paywalled.** Every safety feature is free forever, for everyone. This is an ethics position and it is also the single best marketing claim in the category.
3. **Naira-native, low-ticket, non-recurring by default.** A ₦2,500/month pass that people can pay by transfer, or a wallet top-up, converts in Nigeria where a $9.99 card subscription does not.
4. **Density before breadth.** One saturated zone beats five empty ones. A dating app with 40 people within 10km is dead regardless of how good the code is.
5. **Android-first, data-light, offline-tolerant.** Assume a Tecno/Infinix/itel on 4G that drops to H.

## 3. Architecture

**Stack (fastest path for you, given the Node/JS background):**

- **Client:** Expo React Native + TypeScript. One codebase for both stores, **OTA updates** via EAS so you don't wait on store review to fix a bug.
- **Server:** Node + TypeScript (Fastify). Already your wheelhouse.
- **Data:** PostgreSQL (+ PostGIS for geo) and Redis for the deck queue and rate limits.
- **Realtime chat:** Socket.IO or Supabase Realtime. Store messages, don't proxy them through a third party.
- **Media:** Cloudflare R2 for storage, server-side compression + Opus transcoding for voice notes.
- **Verification:** Dojah or Prembly (NIN/BVN lookup, liveness selfie, phone validation).
- **Payments:** Paystack primary (cards, transfer, USSD, airtime), Flutterwave fallback.
- **Push:** Expo Notifications over FCM.
- **Moderation:** NSFW image classifier feeding a human review queue. **Trust & Safety ops is headcount, not a feature flag.**

**Why distance reads "12 mins away" not "4.2 km":** in Lagos, kilometres are meaningless and traffic is everything. Compute drive-time, not crow-flies. It is a small thing that makes the product feel local instead of translated.

## 4. Trust architecture: three layers, not one

Device fingerprinting and identity verification are different products that people constantly
confuse, and the confusion is expensive. Love Market needs three layers, and only one of them
earns a public badge.

**Layer 1, Identity (who you are).** Phone OTP, NIN/BVN lookup, liveness selfie, via Dojah or
Prembly. This is the only layer that produces the "Verified" badge users actually see.

**Layer 2, Device (how many of you there are).** A persistent device identifier (Fingerprint,
fingerprint.com, which ships a React Native SDK; the free open-source FingerprintJS covers web
only). This catches the real local pattern: one handset running twenty profiles, and a banned
user back the same afternoon. Use it to rank down, to force re-verification, and to flag for
review. Never auto-ban on device ID alone, and never surface it to users.

Two caveats that are specific to this market:

- Fingerprinting assumes one person equals one device. In Nigeria, handsets get shared inside
  families and among friends, so a shared device is weak evidence on its own. Treat it as a
  signal, not a verdict.
- Disclose it in the privacy policy in plain language, and give users a "devices on this
  account" screen they can review and revoke. Being caught doing this silently is worse than
  the fraud you were preventing.

**Layer 3, Graph (is this a ring).** Reports, message patterns, photo reuse detection via
perceptual hashes across profiles, shared payment instruments, and OSINT lookups run by your
Trust & Safety analysts on accounts that have already been reported. Back-office tooling, never
a signup step.

### On tools like fingerprint.to specifically

fingerprint.to is not device fingerprinting. It is OSINT reverse lookup: given a username, email
or phone number it sweeps 700+ platforms and returns matching accounts. It is built for private
and fraud investigators, and it is a real tool. It does not belong in your signup funnel.

- **It does not verify anything.** Their own FAQ: "A search match is a research lead; it does
  not confirm a person's identity." You would be making trust decisions on a signal the vendor
  explicitly disclaims.
- **The signal is adversarially inverted.** The service has an opt-out. A scammer operating at
  scale removes themselves; an ordinary user in Surulere has never heard of it. You would filter
  out honest users and keep the professionals.
- **It is disproportionate under NDPA 2023.** Sweeping a new user's cross-platform footprint to
  decide whether they are real is profiling far beyond the purpose, and you cannot map data
  provenance because the provider queries third parties. That is a DPIA you will lose.
- **It is the wrong story.** "Love Market looks up your whole internet history when you sign up"
  is a headline that ends a dating app whose whole pitch is women's safety.
- **The API is not live.** It is a waitlist.

Where the capability does belong: in your Trust & Safety back office, pointed at reported
accounts, run by a human analyst, with a documented lawful basis and an audit log. That is a
legitimate fraud-investigation use. Pointing it at every new signup is not.

**The rule to hold:** only Layer 1 earns a badge a user can see. Layers 2 and 3 change ranking,
trigger review, and quietly protect the market.

### 4.1 How you actually get NIN verification

You do not get NIMC access. Nobody outside a licensed circle does, and anyone promising you a
direct NIMC integration is selling you something they cannot deliver. The path is fixed:

1. **Be a real Nigerian company.** CAC registration, TIN, and an actual physical presence in
   Nigeria. NINAuth's access criteria require Nigerian presence and being subject to Nigerian law.
2. **Be NDPA compliant first.** Register with the NDPC as a data controller, appoint a DPO,
   publish a privacy policy. This is a stated eligibility requirement for NINAuth access, not a
   later chore. You cannot get access while non-compliant.
3. **Contract an approved NINAuth Verification Partner.** NIMC publishes the approved partner
   list; you pick one and sign with them, and they notify NIMC on your behalf. Expect a decision
   in 5 to 7 business days after contracting. Pricing is negotiated with the partner directly.
4. **Build the consent flow.** NINAuth is consent based: the user explicitly authorises the
   match. NIMC requires informed consent, purpose limitation, logging and monitoring, compliance
   reporting, and accepting independent audits of your use.
5. **Pair the NIN with a liveness check and a face match.** This is the step people skip and it
   is the one that matters. A NIN match proves a real Nigerian with that name exists. It does not
   prove the face in the profile photos belongs to them. Without liveness plus face match, a
   scammer just uses a relative's NIN and a stolen photo set.
6. **Never store the raw NIN.** Keep a token or hash, a verified-at timestamp, and which partner
   ran the check. The NIN itself is not yours to keep.

Vendors that sell this through their partner status: Dojah, Prembly (IdentityPass), Smile ID,
Youverify, VerifyMe. Published entry pricing in 2026 runs about $0.04 per check at the cheap end
(Dojah) to $0.12 (Prembly); Smile ID and Youverify are quote based.

**What it costs at scale.** A NIN lookup is a cheap database query. Liveness and face match is
the expensive layer. Budget roughly $0.24 to $0.42 per verified user end to end, which at the CBN
rate of about ₦1,347 to the dollar (2 Nov 2026) is **₦320 to ₦570 per verified user**:

| Verified users | One-time verification cost |
|---|---|
| 5,000 | $1,200 to $2,100 (₦1.6m to ₦2.8m) |
| 50,000 | $12,000 to $21,000 (₦16m to ₦28m) |
| 100,000 | $24,000 to $42,000 (₦32m to ₦57m) |

At 100,000 users that is roughly three months of your entire subscription revenue, spent once,
before anyone has paid you anything. That is why verification must be risk based: verify when a
user becomes swipeable or asks for the badge, not casually, and re-verify only when the device
layer flags something.

**The tailwind.** The NIMC Act 2026, signed 26 June 2026, repeals the 2007 Act and makes the NIN
the foundation of Nigeria's digital identity, with Section 26 expanding mandatory NIN use across
banking, telecoms and other transactions. Nigerians are being pushed toward NIN verification
everywhere. Your check stops being an odd thing a dating app asks for.

### 4.2 Social handles: stage them, do not ban them

People want to exchange Instagram and X handles because in Lagos a person's social presence *is*
their credibility. Refusing to let anyone check is asking people to meet blind. But the exchange
is also the single most documented scam mechanism in the category.

The evidence is blunt. The FTC found 40% of romance scam losses started on social media and that
scammers "quickly moved the sweet talk to WhatsApp, Google Chat, or Telegram." ICE lists
"requests to move conversations to a separate app" as a headline red flag. Sextortion reports are
up more than eightfold since 2019, and the US and Nigeria are actively cooperating on sextortion
and romance scam enforcement right now. A Nigerian dating app that accelerates the off-platform
handoff is building the on-ramp for the exact crime its own safety story promises to stop.

So: gate it on trust, do not forbid it.

- **No handles in the profile or bio.** That turns the deck into a follower farm, which is the
  single most common complaint about Hinge's social links.
- **Unlock handle exchange after a trust threshold:** both parties verified, a minimum number of
  messages over a minimum number of days, and at least one completed in-app voice or video call.
  The call is the real gate, because it is the one thing a scammer working from stolen photos
  cannot do.
- **Do not design around OAuth linking.** Instagram Basic Display API, the thing that let Hinge
  prove a user owned their Instagram, was permanently shut down on 4 December 2024. There is no
  consumer Instagram API any more; the replacement requires the user to hold a professional
  account. X's API is paid and expensive. The "link your socials to prove you're real" feature
  that was easy in 2021 is not buildable for ordinary users in 2026.
- **If you accept a declared handle, run a proof-of-ownership challenge** (post a code in the bio
  or story, you check it) and label it "self-declared, not verified." Never let a pasted handle
  earn the verified badge, or the badge means nothing.
- **When a handle is shared, log it** and show a one-line interstitial with the scam script
  warning. Keep report and block working on off-platform conduct.

### 4.3 What the back office does not do

The Trust & Safety back office does not verify anyone. It investigates the small share of users
who get reported, and it is reactive, human and slow on purpose. At 100,000 monthly active users
and a typical 2% monthly report rate you are looking at about 2,000 cases a month, and a trained
analyst working properly closes roughly 40 a day, so about **three analysts plus a lead**. It
cannot touch every user, and it must not try to.

Verification of everyone is Layer 1: automated, per head, cheap, at signup. The back office
protects the market; Layer 1 verifies the individual. Confusing the two is how founders try to
staff their way to trust and go bankrupt doing it. The back office is a permanent cost centre
with no revenue attached. Budget for it like a product team and expect it to grow with MAU.

## 5. Monetisation (and the part Tinder can't copy)

Revenue lines, ranked by when they turn on:

**Phase 1, individual premium**
- **Market Pass**, unlimited likes, see who liked you, rewind, 5 Super Likes/week, Passport. ₦2,500/mo · ₦6,500/quarter · ₦19,000/year.
- **Spotlight boost**, ₦700 for 30 minutes at the top of the deck.
- **Super Like**, ₦200 each.
- **Love Market Cash wallet**, ₦1,000 → 1,100 · ₦5,000 → 6,000 · ₦20,000 → 26,000 credits. The wallet is the real trick: it decouples you from the failed-card problem and the "why is this app charging me again" fear.

**Phase 2, Àṣà events (the moat)**
Ticketed singles mixers, Lagos → Abuja → Port Harcourt. ₦15,000 to ₦40,000 per head, sponsor-funded. This is in-person, it is a real date delivered, and a global app will never run it.

**Phase 3, Owambe marketplace (where the money actually is)**
Nigerian courtship spending ends in a wedding, and that budget is enormous. Route matched, long-term couples to vetted vendors (photographers, planners, aso-ebi, caterers) at 10 to 15% commission. The dating app is the customer-acquisition channel for the wedding economy. This is the line that turns a small subscription business into a big one.

**Unit economics to hold yourself to:** blended CAC under ₦1,500, Market Pass conversion ≥ 4% of MAU, and LTV:CAC above 3 within the first two cities. If airtime/wallet payments don't clear 30% of transactions, your pricing is wrong for the market, revisit.

## 6. Build sequence

Each phase should end with something a real person in Lagos can use.

### Phase 0, Foundations (week 1)
- CAC registration, TIN, corporate bank account. Apple/Google org accounts need a D-U-N-S number, start early.
- **NDPA 2023 compliance:** register with NDPC as a data controller, appoint a DPO, run a DPIA. Dating data (photos + location + identity + relationships) is about as sensitive as consumer data gets. Do this before you collect a single profile, not after.
- Pick verification and payment vendors, get sandbox keys.

### Phase 1, The deck works (weeks 2 to 5)
- Auth by phone number (OTP), 18+ hard gate.
- Profile creation: 4 photos, 3 prompts, intention tag, area.
- Verification flow: NIN/BVN + liveness → `verified` badge.
- Swipe deck with PostGIS radius + Redis queue, drive-time distance.
- Report/block on every surface. Moderation queue from day one.

### Phase 2, People can actually talk (weeks 6 to 9)
- Match → chat, with **voice notes** and photo messages.
- In-app audio call (WebRTC). Keep people off WhatsApp until trust is established.
- Safety kit: date check-in, live location share to a trusted contact, "I'm safe" ping.
- Push notifications (match, message), batched and quiet-hours aware.

### Phase 3, Money (weeks 10 to 12)
- Paystack integration: cards, transfer, USSD, airtime.
- Wallet + credits ledger (double-entry, idempotent webhooks).
- Market Pass subscription, Spotlight, Super Likes.
- **Do not hold customer float yourself.** Use a partner so you are not in PSSP-licence territory.

### Phase 4, Launch city #1 (weeks 13 to 16)
- Soft launch: **Lekki/Ajah + Yaba/Surulere** in Lagos. 5,000 verified humans, hand-seeded via campus ambassadors (UNILAG, Yabatech) and night-life partnerships.
- Waitlist with referral position, "Founding 5,000" badge.
- Subsidise verified women (6 months of Market Pass free) until the ratio is healthy. If the ratio is bad, men churn out and you never recover.

### Phase 5, Scale cities + Phase 2/3 revenue
- Abuja (Wuse/Gwarinpa), then Port Harcourt. Repeat the playbook.
- First Àṣà event. Vendor marketplace pilot.

## 7. Go-to-market, Nigerian specifics

- **Distribution:** X/Twitter NG is the cultural engine. TikTok skits. Instagram meme pages. Big Brother Naija season is the single biggest dating-adjacent moment of the year, budget for it.
- **Message in ads:** lead with *"Every profile is a real Nigerian."* Trust, not horniness. It is the only ad Tinder cannot run.
- **Referrals:** friend-links with credits on both sides. Nigeria runs on WhatsApp groups; make the share a WhatsApp-ready card.
- **The founder story:** "Ọjà Ìfẹ́" is a Lagos name. Lean on it. A local brand beats a translated one.

---

## 8. Risks and the honest constraints

- **Same-sex matching is a legal minefield in Nigeria (SSMPA 2013).** You have to make a decision here explicitly, and it needs a lawyer, not an engineer. Treat it as a business/legal call you make on purpose, and know it caps Western app-store and investor narratives.
- **Trust & Safety is a permanent cost centre.** Underfund it and one kidnapping story ends the company. Fund it like a product team.
- **Cold start is the real killer.** Code is the easy part. The first city's density is the whole game.
- **NDPA penalties** are real, and dating data is exactly the kind regulators care about.
- **Verification friction** will cost you signups. Accept it, measure it, keep it. Friction is the feature.

## 9. Open questions for you (decisions, not tasks)

1. **Positioning:** serious relationships first (larger, safer, monetises via weddings) or casual (faster growth, worse ratio, Tinder's war)? This changes the entire design.
2. **Legal posture on same-sex matching**, needs your call and a Nigerian lawyer's.
3. **First city and your access to it**, do you have a way to seed Lagos density (campus/event/friend network), or do we go where you do?
4. **Build vs. buy on the app shell**, Expo from scratch is ~12 weeks to Phase 3; a whitelabel dating backend could cut that but you'd rent your differentiation.
